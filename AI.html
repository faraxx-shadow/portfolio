INTRODUCTION TO AI

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science that lets machines copy human skills like learning, thinking, and solving problems. Instead of following strict step-by-step rules for every action, AI systems use data and math models to spot patterns and make choices on their own

CORE-CONCEPT

Machine Learning (ML): A part of AI where computers look at data to learn and get better over time without being told how by a programmer.

Deep Learning: An advanced type of machine learning that uses big layers of math functions (neural networks) to handle hard jobs like recognizing faces or speech.

Deep Learning: An advanced type of machine learning that uses big layers of math functions (neural networks) to handle hard jobs like recognizing faces or speech.

Natural Language Processing (NLP): Tech that lets computers read, write, and talk in normal human language, used in web translation and voice helpers.

Generative AI: Systems that build new text, images, or music from patterns they found in training data

TYPES OF AI

Narrow AI: Built to do one specific job, like weather apps, spam filters, or chess programs (this is what we use today).

General AI: A future, human-level AI that can learn any intellectual task across many fields (still theoretical).

Superintelligent AI: A hypothetical future stage where machines become much smarter than humans.

DAILY USES OF AI

Daily Life: Phone navigation apps, smart home speakers, and movie recommendation lists.

Work and Business: Automated factory systems, customer support chat tools, and fraud checks for banks.

Science and Health: Finding new medical drugs, reading X-rays, and predicting crop growth.
